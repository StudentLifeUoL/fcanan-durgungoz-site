About

Dr. Fatma Canan Durgungöz is an Assistant Professor (Dr. Öğr. Üyesi) in the Department of Special Education, Faculty of Education, at Mersin University, and an Honorary Research Fellow in the Department of Psychology at the University of Bath, following a postdoctoral fellowship there. She holds a PhD in Special and Inclusive Education from the University of Nottingham.

Her research centres on developmental language disorder (DLD), special educational needs and disabilities (SEND), and the thoughtful use of digital and AI-based tools in special education. Recent work has explored emotion-recognition interventions for children with DLD and their parents, technology-mediated participation for neurodivergent students in higher education, and AI-assisted tools for special education teaching.

She also directs the Turkish side of EDAR, a British Council-funded research capacity-building partnership supporting early-career academics affected by the 2023 Türkiye earthquakes, and has led volunteer efforts to rebuild school libraries in earthquake-affected regions (see Public Engagement).

Publications

  1. Exploring effortless AI-generated gamified quizzes in an online special education module

    Durgungoz, F. C., & Durgungoz, A. · Education and Information Technologies · 2025

    Tested whether AI-generated quiz questions could make an online special education course more engaging and help flag struggling students early. Most AI-written questions were usable with light editing, and low quiz scores closely tracked lower exam performance — pointing to a low-cost way to spot students who need extra support.

  2. "Interactive lessons are great, but too much is too much": Hearing out neurodivergent students, Universal Design for Learning and the case for integrating more anonymous participation options

    Durgungoz, F. C., & Durgungoz, A. · Higher Education · 2025

    Examined how neurodivergent students experience classroom participation, and whether Universal Design for Learning can make lessons more inclusive. Anonymous, technology-mediated ways of taking part — like digital polling — helped neurodivergent students engage without the social pressure of open discussion.

    Read the paper →

  3. An interactive technology-based emotion recognition intervention for children with developmental language disorder: A longitudinal mixed-method study

    Durgungoz, F. C., & St Clair, M. C. · European Journal of Special Needs Education · 2024

    Tested a flexible, technology-based, parent-led programme to help children with developmental language disorder learn to recognise emotions. Found meaningful gains in reading emotions from vocal cues, and reductions in emotional difficulties among participating children.

    Read the paper →

  4. Enhancing Parental Understanding of Emotions in Children with Developmental Language Disorder: An Online Parent-Led Intervention Program

    Durgungoz, F. C., & St Clair, M. C. · Healthcare · 2024

    The first study of its kind, testing whether an online, parent-led programme could shift how parents understand and support their child's emotional development in developmental language disorder. Found a clear, lasting increase in parents' engagement with their child's emotional world.

    Read the paper →

  5. "We are much closer here": exploring the use of WhatsApp as a learning environment in a secondary school mathematics class

    Durgungoz, F. C., & Durgungoz, A. · Learning Environments Research · 2021

    Followed a secondary-school WhatsApp group between a maths teacher and his students over two years to understand how informal messaging shaped learning outside class. The teacher's warm, informal communication style — more than the technology itself — built trust and encouraged students to keep asking for help.

    Read the paper →

  6. Exploring the Use of Touchscreen Devices for Children with Special Educational Needs and Disabilities: Digital vs Traditional

    Durgungoz, F. C., & Durgungoz, A. · European Journal of Special Education Research · 2021

    Compared touchscreen tablets against traditional teaching materials for children with special educational needs. Tablets held children's attention longer, but engagement varied widely by app — success depended heavily on how thoughtfully each app was designed for the child's needs.

    Read the paper →

  7. Decision Making of Speech and Language Therapists: Science or Intuition?

    Durgungoz, F. C., & Emerson, A. · International Journal of Disability, Development and Education · 2021

    Investigated how speech and language therapists actually decide on treatment, across a spectrum from analytical, evidence-based reasoning to fast clinical intuition. Decisions shifted dynamically depending on the case, the therapy type, and family input, rather than following one fixed method.

    Read the paper →

  8. Engaging with parents in decision-making: The dilemma of the ideal and reality

    Durgungoz, F. C., & Emerson, A. · Speech, Language and Hearing · 2021

    Explored the gap between therapists' stated wish to involve parents in their child's speech and language therapy and what actually happens in session. Found real instances of parents being sidelined, and argued that closing the gap needs better parent education alongside greater therapist awareness of their own assumptions.

    Read the paper →

  9. Relying on informal sources in speech and language therapy: why and how?

    Durgungoz, F. C. · European Journal of Special Education Research · 2021

    Asked why speech and language therapists often lean on experience and instinct rather than formal, standardised tools. Found recurring patterns — including distrust of standardised tests and heavy reliance on trial and error — suggesting formal and informal knowledge need to be understood together, not treated as opposites.

    Read the paper →

Public Engagement

Alongside her research, Dr. Durgungöz has led Mersin University Faculty of Education student volunteer efforts to build school libraries in earthquake-affected regions of Türkiye — including Hatay (Samandağ), Gaziantep (İslahiye), and Kahramanmaraş (Andırın) — through the Faculty's community service programme, with each library dedicated to those lost in the 2023 earthquakes.

In recognition of this work, she received Mersin University's 2026 Social Contribution Award (Toplumsal Katkı Ödülü).
